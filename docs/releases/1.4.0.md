# EdgeBeat 1.4.0

## Highlights

- Smoothed the core audio-reactive glow with frame-rate-aware attack and release
  timing, including a lighter 30 Hz path for Low Power Mode.
- The Now Playing ellipsis now exposes copy, recent tracks, and the current
  queue source. Recent tracks are actionable and open in the matching music
  service, falling back to a search when a direct track link is unavailable.
- The update checker now shows the latest release title and release notes when
  a newer version is available.
- Improved lyrics reliability by falling back to an LRCLIB search when exact
  player metadata or duration does not match, and added a clear waiting state
  when a player reports no usable track title.

## Platform note

EdgeBeat uses a read-only Core Audio tap for lighting analysis. It does not
intercept or alter the audio sent to your speakers. Its lighting response is a
visual treatment only, not headphone spatialization or Dolby Atmos.

Real audible processing would require routing the player through a virtual device such as [BlackHole](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole) or [Loopback](https://rogueamoeba.com/loopback/), then processing and replaying the stream through an audio engine. [Steam Audio](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/steam-audio) offers an open-source spatializer for such a pipeline. Dolby Atmos encoding is separate from spatial rendering: its object-based stream cannot be recovered from a stereo mix, and shipping Dolby technology in an app requires evaluating [Dolby's licensing](https://professional.dolby.com/licensing/).

The public [Atmos-Encoder](https://github.com/DRX-Lab/Atmos-Encoder) project requires Dolby Encoding Engine binaries and accepts Atmos sources; the [dolby-atmos-encoder PoC](https://github.com/raress96/dolby-atmos-encoder) documents the same hardware and proprietary-tool limitations. Neither is a drop-in live converter for Spotify or Apple Music.
